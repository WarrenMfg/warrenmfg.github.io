<section id="summary" class="section"><div class="sectionhead"><span class="eyebrow">THE EXECUTIVE SUMMARY</span><h2>AI makes building easier.<br>Does it make innovation better?</h2></div><div class="prose summary-copy"><p>An impressive AI demo answers “Can we build this?” Innovation asks a harder question: “Should this become a product, and what would persuade us otherwise?” The difference is a repeatable way to turn curiosity into testable claims, and testable claims into accountable investment.</p>
<p>The opportunity is substantial, but conditional. In a randomized experiment with 453 professionals, ChatGPT reduced time on specified writing tasks by <strong>40%</strong> and increased rated output quality by <strong>18%</strong>; those results concern bounded writing tasks, not enterprise-wide productivity or profit. (<a target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" href="https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.adh2586">Noy &amp; Zhang, 2023</a>) In a published field study of 5,172 customer-support agents, an AI assistant increased issues resolved per hour by <strong>15% on average</strong>, with substantial differences by worker experience and skill; the staggered-rollout study was not a randomized trial. (<a target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" href="https://academic.oup.com/qje/article/140/2/889/7990658">Brynjolfsson et al., 2025</a>)</p>
<p>There is an equally important counterweight. A meta-analysis of 106 experimental studies found that human-AI teams performed somewhat worse, on average, than whichever was better alone: the human or the AI. Because the studies measured different tasks, the authors report the gap in standard deviations rather than percentages: Hedges’ g = <strong>−0.23</strong>, a small effect, with a 95% confidence interval of −0.39 to −0.07. The whole interval sits below zero, so the shortfall is modest but unlikely to be zero; it is not a 23% productivity decline. (<a target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" href="https://www.nature.com/articles/s41562-024-02024-1">Vaccaro et al., 2024</a>) A 2026 reanalysis of 74 relevant studies identified feedback and human learning as promising moderators, but explicitly called for experiments before drawing stronger causal conclusions. (<a target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42766752/">Berger et al., 2026</a>)</p>
</div><div class="study-strip" role="group" aria-label="Three findings that motivate the framework"><article><b class="stat">40%</b><p>Less time on specified writing tasks; rated quality increased 18%. Randomized experiment, 453 professionals.</p><a href="https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.adh2586" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Noy & Zhang, 2023</a></article><article><b class="stat">15%</b><p>More issues resolved per hour in a support-agent field study. Staggered rollout, 5,172 agents.</p><a href="https://academic.oup.com/qje/article/140/2/889/7990658" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Brynjolfsson et al., 2025</a></article><article><b class="stat">−0.23</b><p>Human-AI teams vs. the better of human or AI alone: a small shortfall of 0.23 standard deviations, not 23%. 95% confidence interval −0.39 to −0.07.</p><a href="https://www.nature.com/articles/s41562-024-02024-1" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Vaccaro et al., 2024</a></article></div><div class="prose summary-copy"><p>The Innovation Loop turns that tension into four practical movements:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Frame together:</strong> Select a consequential user problem, name its eventual owner, and compare AI with the current workflow and a credible simpler alternative.</li>
<li><strong>Test the uncertainty:</strong> Write down what success and failure mean before prototyping. Test the riskiest assumption, not just the easiest capability to demonstrate.</li>
<li><strong>Commit and deliver:</strong> Make an explicit stop, revise, pilot, or scale decision. Transfer operational responsibility through shared work and acceptance, not a presentation alone.</li>
<li><strong>Learn and compound:</strong> Measure actual use, quality, cost, and harm. Feed failures into evaluations and teach the method using evidence, including stopped bets.</li>
</ul>
<p>The aim is not to maximize ideas, demos, approvals, or even launches. It is to make better decisions about which AI-enabled experiences deserve users, resources, and continued operation.</p>
<p>This is not an argument for more committees. It is a proposal for four movements, three living records, and five rules, usable by a small team and extensible to enterprise governance. The practical invitation is to try one bounded opportunity, preserve the evidence, and evaluate the framework itself rather than assuming the framework works.</p>
</div></section>

<section id="framework" class="section"><div class="sectionhead"><span class="eyebrow">THE FRAMEWORK</span><h2>Increase commitment as evidence improves and keep ownership continuous from discovery to real use.</h2></div><div class="prose"><p>The framework has four parts:</p>
<ul>
<li><a href="#movements">The movements</a> the work passes through</li>
<li><a href="#rules">The rules</a> that hold throughout</li>
<li><a href="#accountabilities">The people</a> accountable for it</li>
<li><a href="#records">The records</a> that carry the evidence</li>
</ul></div>
<h3 id="movements">Four movements, not four departments</h3><div class="prose"><p>Together, the four movements form the loop: "Learn and compound" feeds back into "Frame together", and work can return to an earlier movement whenever evidence changes the problem, solution, or investment case. Tidd and Bessant likewise describe innovation as a repeated cycle rather than a neatly linear event. (<a target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" href="https://www.wiley.com/en-us/Managing+Innovation:+Integrating+Technological,+Market+and+Organizational+Change,+8th+Edition-p-9781394252060">Tidd &amp; Bessant, 2024, ch. 3</a>)</p></div>
<div class="movements">
<div class="movement"><span class="movement-num">01</span><h4>Frame together</h4><p class="movement-question">Is this a problem worth testing?</p><p class="movement-evidence"><span>Minimum evidence to move forward</span>Named user and owner, observable problem, baseline, riskiest assumption, authorized experiment</p><details id="phase-1"><summary>How to run it</summary><div class="prose"><p><strong>Purpose:</strong> Find an important uncertainty worth paying to reduce. Begin with the product or service owner, not with a finished concept looking for a home.</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Discover the work:</strong> Observe users, examine workflow data and existing complaints, identify workarounds, and distinguish the expressed request from the underlying task. Record access limits and whose perspective is missing.</li>
<li><strong>Form alternatives:</strong> Generate candidate approaches independently before pooling them; include a process change, a deterministic solution, or an existing product when plausible. Use AI to widen and challenge the set, not to create a false impression of independent agreement.</li>
<li><strong>Define the test:</strong> Select a primary user outcome, a baseline, a credible comparator, a failure condition, and the cheapest ethical test of the highest-consequence assumption. Establish authorized data use and the boundaries of the experiment.</li>
</ul>
<p>The independent-ideation suggestion is a design precaution, not a proven optimal workshop format. It responds to an experiment in short-story writing where AI assistance improved individual evaluations but made the stories more similar to each other; that finding does not establish that AI reduces product-innovation diversity in every setting. (<a target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11244532/">Doshi &amp; Hauser, 2024</a>)</p>
<p><strong>Exit decision:</strong> The outcome owner and delegated investment authority authorize a bounded experiment, revise the opportunity, or stop. The opportunity card should be understandable without a slide deck.</p>
</div></details></div>
<div class="movement"><span class="movement-num">02</span><h4>Test the uncertainty</h4><p class="movement-question">Does the proposed solution merit exposure to users?</p><p class="movement-evidence"><span>Minimum evidence to move forward</span>Prototype evidence against predefined criteria, cost range, failure analysis, bounded pilot plan</p><details id="phase-2"><summary>How to run it</summary><div class="prose"><p><strong>Purpose:</strong> Produce evidence that can change the decision. Refine the proposal and build the prototype together rather than requiring a detailed business case before learning anything.</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Write the evaluation contract:</strong> Define the population, unit of analysis, current workflow, comparator, primary measure, meaningful improvement, guardrails, sample rationale, and analysis/stopping plan. Separate qualitative discovery from confirmatory claims.</li>
<li><strong>Build the smallest useful test:</strong> Use a mock-up, concierge workflow, offline model comparison, retrieval experiment, or bounded working prototype according to the uncertainty. Avoid unnecessary agents, integrations, or model training.</li>
<li><strong>Evaluate the whole experience:</strong> Test task completion, human correction effort, unacceptable errors, latency, and total cost, not just model output. Where appropriate and safe, compare human-only, AI-only, and human-AI performance on the same task.</li>
<li><strong>Challenge the result:</strong> Use representative cases and an untouched final test set; examine relevant user and task slices. For temporal predictions, respect time ordering and prevent leakage; for generative systems, include groundedness, abstention, and adversarial cases.</li>
<li><strong>Update the investment case:</strong> Show downside, base, and upside scenarios, with assumptions for adoption, integration, review, inference, maintenance, and error costs. Distinguish a forecast from an observed benefit.</li>
</ul>
<p>Application-specific evaluation, factual consistency, cost, and latency are explicit concerns in Huyen’s AI engineering treatment; NIST also cautions that laboratory and benchmark performance may not extrapolate to real use. (<a target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" href="https://www.oreilly.com/library/view/ai-engineering/9781098166298/ch04.html">Huyen, 2025, ch. 4</a>, <a target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" href="https://tsapps.nist.gov/publication/get_pdf.cfm?pub_id=958388">NIST, 2024</a>) The statistical-learning textbook provides the methodological foundation for model assessment, resampling, and multiple testing, rather than a universal sample size for this framework. (<a target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" href="https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-3-031-38747-0">James et al., 2023</a>)</p>
<p><strong>Exit decision:</strong> Stop, revise, or authorize a bounded pilot. An inconclusive result calls for an explicit choice about whether more information is worth its cost, not an automatic declaration of success or failure.</p>
</div></details></div>
<div class="movement"><span class="movement-num">03</span><h4>Commit and deliver</h4><p class="movement-question">Can we responsibly own this in use?</p><p class="movement-evidence"><span>Minimum evidence to move forward</span>Explicit investment decision, accepted ownership, risk review, monitoring and rollback</p><details id="phase-3"><summary>How to run it</summary><div class="prose"><p><strong>Purpose:</strong> Turn a supported proposition into an owned, controlled product or service. A good demo alone is not evidence that the organization can operate it.</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Present the decision, not the theater:</strong> State the problem, alternatives, strongest evidence and counterevidence, uncertainty, economics, risks, and precise resource request. Use an existing product review; require an executive presentation only when the decision exceeds delegated authority.</li>
<li><strong>Pilot with controls:</strong> Define who is exposed, what the system may do, the monitoring owner, incident route, and conditions for pause or rollback. Use shadow evaluation or controlled release where appropriate; shadow mode does not test every user-interaction effect.</li>
<li><strong>Accept together:</strong> The owner, builders, and relevant reviewers walk through the code or prototype, data rights and lineage, prompts/model versions, evaluations, known failures, instrumentation, support, and dependencies.</li>
<li><strong>Commit to delivery capacity:</strong> Name the delivery team and funded roadmap or backlog commitment. If another team will continue the work, require explicit acceptance and overlap; a handoff is not complete because a document was sent.</li>
</ul>
<p>For agentic services, restrict tool permissions, isolate tests, log actions, and require appropriate human authorization for consequential actions. These are risk controls informed by NIST’s treatment of security, prompt injection, monitoring, and human-AI configurations, not a claim that a checklist can guarantee safety. (<a target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" href="https://tsapps.nist.gov/publication/get_pdf.cfm?pub_id=958388">NIST, 2024</a>)</p>
<p><strong>Exit decision:</strong> The owner accepts the bounded service; the investment authority approves the relevant commitment; required risk reviewers approve within their remit. Unknowns stay visible on the ownership card, with an owner and next review.</p>
</div></details></div>
<div class="movement"><span class="movement-num">04</span><h4>Learn and compound</h4><p class="movement-question">Did value survive real use?</p><p class="movement-evidence"><span>Minimum evidence to move forward</span>Measured outcomes, adverse effects, operating cost, next decision, reusable learning</p><details id="phase-4"><summary>How to run it</summary><div class="prose"><p><strong>Purpose:</strong> Establish whether the proposition produces durable value in use, and improve both the service and the method. Delivery is a beginning of measurement, not the end of innovation.</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Measure in use:</strong> Compare outcomes with the baseline or a suitable control; distinguish availability, exposure, trial, repeat use, and successful task completion. Treat clicks, edits, and satisfaction as signals requiring interpretation, not direct proof of value.</li>
<li><strong>Watch degradation:</strong> Monitor outcome quality, cost, latency, data changes, incidents, and user workarounds. Re-evaluate after changes to data, models, prompts, retrieval, or tools; versioning and continuous monitoring are core MLOps concerns. (<a target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" href="https://arxiv.org/pdf/2205.02302">Kreuzberger et al., 2023</a>)</li>
<li><strong>Retrospect privately and together:</strong> Offer confidential one-to-one feedback as well as a team discussion. Select one or two changes to the next iteration and assign an owner; do not turn the retrospective into a mandatory ritual without an improvement decision.</li>
<li><strong>Teach with evidence:</strong> Use a roadshow containing a successful case, a stopped case, costs, limitations, and reusable evaluations. For train-the-trainer adoption, progress from observation to co-facilitation to independently facilitated work reviewed against the same record requirements.</li>
<li><strong>Govern a portfolio only when needed:</strong> Review strategic fit, evidence strength, shared dependencies, cumulative exposure, operating burden, and resources across bets. Compare within appropriate maturity and risk categories; do not rank an exploratory option against a mature product using a single ROI score.</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Exit decision:</strong> Continue, change, expand, retire, or return to discovery. Keep a subsequent value-review date even when the next facilitation cycle has already begun.</p>
</div></details></div>
</div>
<details class="worked-example"><summary>Worked example: the four movements applied to an AI research brief</summary><div class="prose"><p>Consider a hypothetical service that helps a product manager prepare a source-grounded research brief. The following is an illustration, not a claim about any company’s roadmap, internal practices, data, or achievable results.</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Frame:</strong> Users need a defensible answer to a bounded research question. Compare the current search-and-writing workflow with a structured non-generative template and the AI-assisted proposal; nominate an owner.</li>
<li><strong>Test:</strong> Define success as answering the question with correct, relevant, traceable evidence under a blinded rubric. Measure total time, verification and correction effort, unsupported claims, citation support, latency, and cost per successful brief.</li>
<li><strong>Commit:</strong> Only if the prespecified quality and risk criteria are met, run a bounded pilot with consenting users, clear limits, and an incident route. Do not allow unverified external messages or autonomous consequential actions.</li>
<li><strong>Learn:</strong> Assess repeat use alongside completed, acceptable briefs; return corrections and retrieval failures to the evaluation set. A faster first draft that increases total verification effort may fail the product’s goal.</li>
</ul>
<p>No numerical target is supplied because baseline difficulty, risk tolerance, and sample requirements are unknown. Set those before exposure rather than borrowing an impressive number from an unrelated study.</p>
</div></details><h3 id="rules">Five rules</h3><div class="prose"><ul>
<li><strong>Start with an outcome, not an AI requirement:</strong> State whose task should improve, against what alternative, and why AI might be necessary. “No AI” remains an acceptable answer.</li>
<li><strong>Make claims falsifiable:</strong> Record the hypothesis, comparison, success criteria, guardrails, and stop conditions before reviewing results. Label later exploratory findings as exploratory.</li>
<li><strong>Match permission to risk:</strong> Do not treat approval to explore as approval to expose private data, affect users, or deploy autonomous actions.</li>
<li><strong>Keep an owner from beginning to end:</strong> Name the product or service owner during framing; that owner accepts delivery and remains accountable for outcomes after launch.</li>
<li><strong>Preserve decisions and counterevidence:</strong> Record stopped ideas, negative findings, limitations, and disagreements alongside successes.</li>
</ul>
<p>The rationale for explicit hypotheses comes partly from four randomized trials involving 759 firms: training in a scientific approach increased project termination by 9.8 percentage points in the pooled analysis, rather than simply encouraging more persistence. (<a target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" href="https://openaccess.city.ac.uk/id/eprint/32437/8/Strategic%20Management%20Journal%20-%202024%20-%20Camuffo%20-%20A%20scientific%20approach%20to%20entrepreneurial%20decision%E2%80%90making%20Large%E2%80%90scale.pdf">Camuffo et al., 2024</a>) That supports treating evidence-led stopping as legitimate; it does not establish a desirable termination rate for corporate AI projects.</p>
</div><h3 id="accountabilities">Four accountabilities, not four new job titles</h3><div class="prose">
<ul>
<li><strong>Outcome owner:</strong> Represents user and business needs, sets acceptance criteria with the team, owns the outcome measure, and accepts the service into its product roadmap.</li>
<li><strong>Builders:</strong> Provide the design, domain, data, software, and ML capabilities needed to test and deliver the use case. Invite security, privacy, accessibility, legal, and operations specialists when the use case requires them.</li>
<li><strong>Facilitator:</strong> Protects inquiry, enables cross-functional access, keeps the evidence legible, and runs the retrospective. The facilitator is not the sole owner of the invention or its delivery.</li>
<li><strong>Sponsor or investment authority:</strong> Authorizes resources and risk within delegated limits, resolves cross-functional constraints, and decides larger commitments.</li>
</ul>
<p>A small team may combine accountabilities; higher-risk decisions should receive independent review. As a starting configuration, try four to seven active contributors with named specialist access, then adjust to the work rather than treating headcount as a success condition.</p>
<p>Recruit volunteers for interest where useful, but obtain explicit manager agreement on protected capacity, availability, and replacement coverage. Include different functional expertise and direct access to affected users; do not assume that nominal diversity, volunteer enthusiasm, or membership in a data science function automatically provides AI product delivery capability.</p>
<p>This cross-functional emphasis is consistent with MLOps research identifying business, data science, data engineering, software, and operations roles, together with collaboration and continuous feedback. (<a target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" href="https://arxiv.org/pdf/2205.02302">Kreuzberger et al., 2023</a>)</p>

<p>Use randomized product experiments when feasible and ethical for causal impact claims. If using observational before/after comparisons, identify seasonality, selection, workflow changes, and other rival explanations; do not relabel correlation as incremental value.</p>
<p>For the financial case, use a transparent scenario model: eligible volume × adoption × incremental contribution per adopted task, minus incremental operating and implementation costs. This is a proposed planning identity, not a literature-derived effect estimate; avoid double-counting benefits and do not book released capacity as cash savings unless spending actually changes.</p>

<section id="evidence" class="section"><div class="sectionhead"><span class="eyebrow">THE EVIDENCE</span><h2>Striking results.<br>Visible boundaries.</h2></div><div class="evidence-grid"><article><p><strong>Writing assistance:</strong> 453 professionals; randomized online experiment; 40% less time and 18% higher rated quality on occupation-specific writing tasks. That’s a time saving on specific tasks, not 40% more enterprise productivity or profit. (<a target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" href="https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.adh2586">Noy &amp; Zhang, 2023</a>)</p>
</article><article><p><strong>Customer support:</strong> 5,172 agents; staggered-rollout field study; 15% more issues resolved/hour on average, with a 30% increase for less-skilled and less-experienced workers. The published 2025 result is used here, not the earlier working-paper headline of approximately 14%. (<a target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" href="https://academic.oup.com/qje/article/140/2/889/7990658">Brynjolfsson et al., 2025</a>)</p>
</article><article><p><strong>Product ideation:</strong> A 2026 Organization Science article reports a preregistered experiment with 791 Procter &amp; Gamble professionals; individual AI users improved proposal performance by 0.37 standard deviations over unaided individuals, described by the authors as approximately 9.6%. Outcomes were expert-rated product proposals, not launched products, customer adoption, revenue, or ROI. The 0.37 is in standard deviations, not percent. (<a target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" href="https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/orsc.2025.20702">Dell’Acqua et al., 2026</a>)</p>
</article><article><p><strong>Complementarity:</strong> 106 studies and 370 effect sizes. On average, human-AI teams fell short of whichever was better alone, the human or the AI, by 0.23 standard deviations: a small effect, with a 95% confidence interval of −0.39 to −0.07. The same analysis found that teams often did outperform humans working alone, so “human plus AI never works” would be false. (<a target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" href="https://www.nature.com/articles/s41562-024-02024-1">Vaccaro et al., 2024</a>)</p>
</article><article><p><strong>Learning matters, provisionally:</strong> A 2026 reanalysis examined 74 relevant studies and found feedback plus explanations associated with positive synergy, while explanations without feedback were associated with negative synergy. This is a moderator finding, not randomized proof that adding feedback will create synergy. (<a target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42766752/">Berger et al., 2026</a>)</p>
</article><article><p><strong>Scientific stopping:</strong> Four trials, 759 firms; pooled termination effect +9.8 percentage points. It is not a 9.8% increase in innovation success, a universal kill-rate target, or a test of this framework. (<a target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" href="https://openaccess.city.ac.uk/id/eprint/32437/8/Strategic%20Management%20Journal%20-%202024%20-%20Camuffo%20-%20A%20scientific%20approach%20to%20entrepreneurial%20decision%E2%80%90making%20Large%E2%80%90scale.pdf">Camuffo et al., 2024</a>)</p>
